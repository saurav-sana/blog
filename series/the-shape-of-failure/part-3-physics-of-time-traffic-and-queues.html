The Physics of Time, Traffic, and Queues

Poisson Bursts, Pipeline Delays, and Kingman’s Saturation Trap

What This Part Covers

Traffic does not arrive uniformly. It arrives in bursts governed by the Poisson process — and the gap between the average arrival rate and the peak burst rate is where most capacity plans silently fail. When a system is pushed past 85% utilization, Kingman’s heavy-traffic approximation shows that queue wait time does not grow linearly: it diverges toward infinity.

This part introduces the queueing physics that underpin every latency SLO, every autoscaling policy, and every load balancer configuration in production. It is the mathematical explanation for why your system feels fine at 80% CPU and collapses at 90%.

Key Concepts

  • Poisson arrivals: why traffic bursts are the rule, not the exception
  • Exponential service times and the M/M/1 queue model
  • Kingman’s formula: the 85% utilization cliff and queue wait time divergence
  • Erlang distribution: modeling multi-stage pipeline latency
  • Little’s Law: the relationship between WIP, throughput, and cycle time
