The Mathematics of Reliability and Retries

Why Quorums Lie to You, and the Statistical Truth About Node Failures

September 25, 2026 · The Shape of Failure Series

What This Part Covers

The assumption that three replicas means three independent failure probabilities is one of the most expensive misconceptions in distributed systems engineering. Correlated failures — shared power domains, shared network switches, shared kernel versions — mean the Binomial model breaks down precisely when you need it most.

This part builds the statistical foundation for reasoning about reliability: from a single node’s Bernoulli trial, through quorum consensus under Binomial assumptions, to the Geometric distribution that governs how many retries you actually need before a circuit breaker should trip.

Key Concepts

  • Bernoulli trials and the single-node failure probability
  • Binomial distribution: quorum consensus and the myth of independent replicas
  • Geometric distribution: retry budgets and exponential backoff with jitter
  • Negative Binomial: error budget modeling across a rolling window
  • Weibull distribution: hardware aging curves and memory leak detection
